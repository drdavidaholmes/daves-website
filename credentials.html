Academic Credentials

Recent Select Publications

  • Athletics Weekly · ‘Athletics Through Time’ (July 2025 to present)

    Author of the ongoing historical feature series: 18 instalments to date (approximately 1,000 words each), consistently delivered ahead of deadline. Subjects span the Commonwealth Games, European Championships, political turning points in athletics, Japanese track and field, distance events (marathon, half marathon, steeplechase, cross-country, trail running), relays, the 800m, pole vault, indoor and combined events, and athletics in ancient Greece.

  • Journal of Olympic History · Feature article (2026)

    D. Holmes, ‘Gordon Ingate: Bradman of the Yachting World’, Journal of Olympic History, 02-2026 (forthcoming, Dec 2026).

  • Journal of Olympic History · Book review (2026)

    Review of Michael R. Payne, Fast Tracks and Dark Deals (Chiselbury Publishing, 2025), Journal of Olympic History, Vol. 34, No. 1 (2026), pp. 92–93.

  • Co-author (in progress)

    Holmes, D. A., Benedettucci, F., and Pecchioli, L., Archaeological-historical and architectural research and studies on the Circus of Maxentius, in collaboration with Sovrintendenza Capitolina (Rome), Collana di Studi Mediterranei e Orientali, Espera (book proposal in progress).

  • Chapter author and co-editor (2025)

    Holmes, D. A., ‘The Role of Sport in Ancient Rome During Periods of Adversity and its Implications for Ostia and Portus’, in Pecchioli, L., D’Alessio, A., Meneghini, R., and Holmes, D.A. (eds), Hazard and Disaster Risk: Ostia and Portus Between Hypothesis and Reality (Oxford: BAR Publishing, May 2025).

  • Preface author and chief editor (2023)

    Holmes, D. A., ‘Historical Preface’, in L. Pecchioli, Archaeoseismology: Methods and Case Studies, Natural Science in Archaeology (Geneva: Springer Nature, 2023).

Teaching & scholarship

  • David has taught extensively on the history and politics of sport at several institutions, including the University of New South Wales (UNSW), the University of Sydney, and Charles Sturt University. For over a decade, he has also served as an Adjunct Professor and principal lecturer at Boston University’s Sydney Campus.
